Notes on Sentence Length

Working notes · unlisted

Average sentence length is the first thing anyone reaches for when they want a number that stands in for "style," and it is almost always the wrong number on its own. The mean is dragged around by a handful of very long sentences, so two novels with identical means can feel nothing alike on the page.

The distribution is the more honest object. Plot the sentence lengths of a chapter and you usually get a long right tail: a dense clump between five and twenty words, then a thin scatter running out past sixty. What changes between authors is mostly the shape of that tail, not the center of the clump.
